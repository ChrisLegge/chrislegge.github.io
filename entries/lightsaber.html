Custom Lightsaber

June 2025

Overview

I built a fully working combat lightsaber from scratch. The blade is lit by 188 individually addressable WS2812B LEDs controlled by an Arduino, running animations I wrote myself, and all of the electronics and the battery fit inside a handle I made on workshop tools.

ControllerArduino, programmed in C with the FastLED library
LEDs188 WS2812B, two strips of 94 back to back
ControlsPower switch, on/off control and a colour button
HandlePVC tube, laser cut 3mm acrylic grips
ToolsLathe, belt sander, laser cutter, soldering iron

The Handle

The handle is mainly made from two tubes of PVC pipe, one fitting completely inside the other. After making a design, I cut the PVC with a hacksaw and used a belt sander to get the pieces smooth. For the parts with details or bevels I used the lathe to cut into the PVC evenly and get the details right.

For the grip strips I used a laser cutter and a sheet of 3mm acrylic to get an accurate shape. I then spray painted all the pieces, added some dirt and wear so it looks used, and put it together with a polymer welding glue. I also designed a small cap to cover the bottom so there would be no hole at the end of the pipe.

The Blade

The blade started as a cheap spare from a lightsaber shop. Inside it I put two strips of 94 LEDs, soldered end to end so they act as one strip of 188, and stuck them back to back with a wooden pole through the middle so the blade doesn't collapse. I wrapped the LEDs in packing foam, which works as a cheap diffuser, so the blade glows evenly instead of showing each LED as a dot.

The three wires from the strip (power, ground and data) go to a removable connector, so the blade can come out of the handle.

Electronics & Code

The connector is soldered to an Arduino along with a battery, a switch and a button, and after a lot of testing I managed to fit all of it inside the handle. A switch on the back cuts the battery off completely, and there is a removable panel so the battery can be changed.

Because the strip is folded in half inside the blade, the first and last LEDs both sit at the base and the middle of the strip is at the tip. So every animation lights the LEDs at both ends of the strip together and works inwards towards the middle. That makes the blade look like it is extending out of the handle, and turning it off runs the same thing in reverse.

The button changes the colour of the blade. I have also written a few more animations, like a white band that travels up the blade and a red bleed effect, which I am adding for when the button is pressed and held.

I capped the brightness in code at 100 out of 255. At full white, 188 LEDs could pull over 11 A, which is far too much for a battery inside a handle, so keeping the brightness down matters as much for the power as it does for how it looks.
